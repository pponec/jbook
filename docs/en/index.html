Annotation

We won't stop at syntax. You will apply your knowledge to a series of practical examples leading to an interactive web browser game. You will adopt professional habits: learn to design architecture, create UML diagrams, and write automated tests. All this with an emphasis on clean code and modern trends, including the effective use of artificial intelligence.

 

Who Is This Book For

Thanks to its wide range of topics and low barrier to entry, this book is intended for practically anyone considering a move into the world of IT. It targets readers looking for confidence, structure, and a clear plan, who don't want to waste time sifting through disjointed internet sources. It is ideal for self-taught students who need to see quick results (a functional website) to stay motivated. For all these readers, the book offers an "all-in-one" solution at an affordable price.
The book will be appreciated not only by engineering students but also by enthusiasts from other professions who want to gain sought-after IT skills. It offers a theoretical foundation (memory, algorithms) presented in an entertaining way (games, web). Furthermore, the web-based approach gives you the feeling of creating something real and visible. At the same time, it can serve as inspiration for educators and IT teachers looking for attractive examples for their classes.

 

Why I Wrote This Book

When I started with Java, I felt that a textbook could be written in a more engaging way. After years of practice, I published an article about creating websites in Java using an object model of HTML elements. It occurred to me that this could be a great foundation for interactive examples in teaching. When I later opened a new textbook in a bookstore, I again encountered formal descriptions, black-and-white images, and examples based on the outdated Swing library. That’s why I decided to embark on the uncertain path of an innovative programming textbook author.
